🚄 How the KORAIL Pass actually works: there is no "10-day" pass. You choose a pass of 2, 3, 4, or 5 flexible travel days, and you use those days on any dates you like within a 10-day window that begins on your chosen start date (travel days need not be consecutive). We're using a 5-Day Flexible Saver Pass (for 2 travelling together): the southern loop needs just 4 travel days — Jan 6, 7, 10, 12 (Seoul→Jeonju→Busan→Gyeongju→Seoul), leaving 1 spare, all inside the 10-day window. Reserve seats early — since Oct 2026, KTX seats open two months before departure.
⏱️ Departure-day math: 13:20 flight → be at ICN by ~11:00 → AREX is ~43 min, so leave central Seoul by ~09:30. Jan 14 is your last real day; Jan 15 is airport-only.

💡 Getting around: apps & useful tips

🗺️ Maps & navigationinstall before you fly
  • Naver Map — your primary. Best English support of the Korean apps, dense coverage of restaurants & venues, and excellent subway/bus directions with live timing. Default to this for everything. The map links on this page all open in Naver.
  • Kakao Map — keep as backup. A good cross-check when Naver gives an odd result; some locals rate its walking directions & certain POIs slightly higher.
  • ⚠️ Google Maps is limited in Korea — no car navigation and patchy transit/walking directions (due to local map-data export rules). Fine for previewing a location, not for navigating on the ground.
  • One-liner: Navigate with Naver, hail taxis & message with Kakao.
  • Tip: try searching a spot now (e.g. 명동교자 / Myeongdong Kyoja) so the interface is familiar before you land.
📱 Other essentialscomms · taxis · pay · transit
  • KakaoTalk — Korea's dominant messaging app. A lot of bookings, guesthouses & tour operators communicate through it; handy for your friends too.
  • Kakao T — the standard taxi-hailing app, pairs with your Kakao account.
  • T-money card — tap-to-ride for subway/bus (and many convenience-store purchases); top up at any CU/GS25 or station machine.
  • eSIM / pocket wifi — set up data on arrival at ICN (or buy an eSIM before you fly) so the maps & apps work from the moment you land.
  • Winter note: January in Korea is cold & dry (often below freezing) — phone batteries drain fast in the cold, so carry a power bank for a day of map use.
📶 Staying connecteddata: eSIM beats Three roaming
  • Skip Three's roaming pass for Korea — it works out ~£85pp for 14 days (≈ £170 for the two of you). A travel eSIM does the same job for a fraction of that.
  • Light–normal use → Airalo 10 GB each (~£15pp, ≈ £30 total). Runs on SK Telecom (top-rated coverage); 10 GB comfortably covers 2 weeks of Naver Maps, messaging & browsing.
  • Heavy use / zero worry → Ubigi unlimited, 15 days (~£27pp, ≈ £54 total).
  • Net saving vs Three: roughly £115–£140 for the couple, and no pocket-wifi gadget to carry or return.
  • Before you fly: buy the eSIM online, install the QR code at home, set it to activate on landing. Keep your Three SIM active for your UK number (calls/texts/2FA) — data runs on the eSIM.
  • Check first: both phones must be eSIM-capable & unlocked (recent iPhones & most flagship Androids are). If one isn't, share one eSIM via hotspot or use a pocket wifi for that phone.
  • Our phones: ✅ Pixel 7 & Pixel 6 are both eSIM-ready (all Pixels from the 3 onward are). Just confirm each is network-unlocked with Three before flying, and keep the Three SIM in with data roaming off so data runs on the eSIM.
  • Prices change — confirm on the provider's site when booking.
💳 Money & paymentscards · cash · transit
  • Cards are king. Korea is highly card-friendly — Visa/Mastercard work almost everywhere (shops, restaurants, cafés, taxis). Bring a no/low foreign-fee card (e.g. a travel debit/credit card) to dodge charges.
  • Carry some cash too. Handy for traditional markets (Gwangjang, Jagalchi), street food, small eateries, makgeolli bars & temple donations. Withdraw won from ATMs marked "Global" (at banks, convenience stores, airports).
  • Currency: Korean won (₩). Rough feel: ₩1,000 ≈ 55–60p. Street-food snacks ₩2,000–5,000; a casual meal ₩8,000–15,000pp.
  • T-money card doubles as a tap-to-pay wallet in convenience stores & some taxis — top it up with cash at any CU/GS25.
  • Tipping is not expected — no need to tip in restaurants, taxis or bars. Prices are as marked.
  • Tax refund: on bigger purchases look for "Tax Free" shops — you can claim VAT back at the airport (keep receipts & your passport).
🙇 Etiquette & customsa few helpful norms
  • Shoes off indoors — remove them at temple-stay quarters, some traditional restaurants (floor seating) & guesthouses. Slip-ons make this easy.
  • Two hands — give/receive items, money & especially drinks with two hands (or a hand on the forearm) when with elders — a sign of respect.
  • Pouring drinks — don't pour your own; fill others' glasses and let them fill yours. Turn slightly away from elders when drinking.
  • Keep it low on transit — subways & trains are quiet; keep phone calls brief and voices down. Watch for priority seats (leave them free).
  • Temples: dress modestly, speak softly, don't point feet at Buddha statues, and follow your host's lead during ceremonies.
  • Public manners: little tipping/haggling; queue neatly; bins are scarce — you may carry rubbish a while. A small bow + "annyeonghaseyo" (hello) and "gamsahamnida" (thank you) go a long way.
  • Trying chopsticks & a few words of Korean is warmly appreciated — effort matters more than perfection.

🎟️ Book ahead vs. walk-in

Good news: Korea needs far less advance booking than many places — museums, palaces & temples are nearly all walk-in. Only a handful of things genuinely need booking.
🔴 Must book before you golimited / passport / sells out
  • DMZ tour — passports go on a manifest, military-cleared seats are limited · book ASAP (weeks ahead)
  • KORAIL Pass + KTX seat reservations — buy the pass anytime, but seats open ~2 months before & popular trains fill · reserve ~early Nov 2026
  • Temple stay (if doing the optional Seoul one) — small capacity, limited English programs, apply at eng.templestay.com · 3–4+ weeks ahead
  • Accommodation — Seoul hotel, Jeonju, Busan, Gyeongju; winter + weekends fill · soon
🟡 Smart to pre-booksaves time / money
  • Vivaldi ski day (if doing it) — shuttle + lift/rental package online (Klook/KKday) is cheaper & simpler than on the day
  • Any dated show — K-pop concert, live-music hall, musical or fan event you specifically want can sell out; book the date
  • Nanta / Painters HERO (non-verbal shows) — optional, reserve if keen on a specific night
🟢 Just turn up — no bookingbuy at the gate
  • Gyeongju National Museum — walk-in (often free)
  • Palaces (Gyeongbokgung & co.) — cheap tickets at the gate; free entry in hanbok
  • National Museum of Korea, Folk Museum, Leeum, MMCA, DDP — walk-in, often free
  • Bulguksa & Seokguram, Haedong Yonggungsa, Beomeosa — walk-in
  • Bukchon, Insadong, markets, Gamcheon — free to wander
  • Exception if you add it: Changdeokgung's Secret Garden (Huwon) is guided-only with limited slots — pre-book that one

🧳 Winter packing list

Seoul in January typically sits around −5 to −10 °C (colder with wind, milder in Busan). It's cold and dry, with occasional snow — think layers, wind protection & warm extremities. Tap items to tick them off.
🧥 Clothing — layers
  • Warm waterproof/windproof coat (parka)
  • Thermal base layers (tops & leggings) ×2–3
  • Fleece / knit mid-layers (jumpers)
  • Insulated/wool trousers or lined jeans
  • Warm socks (wool) — several pairs
  • Gloves (touchscreen-friendly)
  • Warm hat / beanie
  • Scarf or neck gaiter
  • Pyjamas / loungewear (warm)
  • A few smart-casual items for nice dinners/bars
  • Modest layers for temple stay (if you do it)
  • Swimwear — for jjimjilbang / Spa Land
👟 Footwear
  • Warm waterproof boots with good grip (icy pavements!)
  • Comfortable walking shoes (lots of walking)
  • Slip-on shoes for spa / temple / indoors
❄️ Cold-weather extras
  • Hand & toe warmers (sold everywhere in KR too)
  • Lip balm & heavy moisturiser (dry winter air)
  • Hydrating hand cream
  • Power bank (cold drains phone batteries fast)

  • Sunglasses (bright, low winter sun on snow)
